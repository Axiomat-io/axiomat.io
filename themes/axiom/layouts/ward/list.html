we're starting from scratch — build an agent that goes out, finds and researches stocks, then makes and tracks predictions against them.

What it does

If you can describe it, Ward can run it.

People point Ward at real, recurring work — watching competitors, tracking predictions, synthesizing research — and at the small stuff they never get to. Each one is an agent that handles it on a schedule and only speaks up when it matters. A few people have built, just by asking:

🔎 A competitor & market monitor

Point it at rivals, a sector, or a set of tickers. Each run it researches what actually changed, writes it up, and keeps a running intelligence brief — the standing report you never have time to compile.

📈 A prediction engine that grades itself

Give it stocks or themes and it makes explicit, falsifiable calls, stores them, and scores itself when they come due — an honest paper track record of its own reasoning. Research only: paper positions, never real trades, and nothing here is financial advice.

📚 A research synthesizer

Hand it a topic and it digs, dedupes against what it's already found, and keeps a living, evidence-first brief — in the app, or synced to Notion where you work.

✉️ Your inbox, triaged

Connect Gmail and it sorts the noise, summarizes the threads that matter, and drafts replies for you to approve before anything sends.

📅 Your calendar, prepped

Connect Google Calendar and it reminds you, preps you for what's next, and helps you get things on the books.

✅ …and the small stuff too

Renewals, warranties, a news digest, the passport that expires next spring. The low-stakes things you keep meaning to track — handled, so they're off your mind.

It's like having a few tireless little assistants who each handle one thing — keeping track for you, staying quiet until it matters, and mostly looking after themselves.

How it works

You have a conversation. It does the rest.

No setup screens, no rules to configure, no code. You chat with Ward like you'd text a capable friend — and it turns what you said into an assistant that quietly gets it done.

Step 1
Just say what you want
YouKeep an eye on my stock watchlist and give me a short read each morning on what moved and why.
YouRemind me before any of my subscriptions renew, and before my passport expires.
YouEvery evening, sum up the important emails from my inbox and draft replies I can approve.

Plain English. Ward figures out what it needs and builds the assistant for you.

Step 2
It runs on its own
Works on a schedule— every morning, every hour, or when something happens. You don't have to be around.
Remembers as it goes— it knows what it already told you, so you rarely get the same thing twice.
Grows into the job— when an assistant needs something new to do its work better, Ward can add it, with little effort from you.

Set it up once. It keeps working in the background — and flags anything that needs your call.

Why Ward

Everything else still leaves the work to you.

There's no shortage of AI. What's rare is something that just handles things in the background, gets more useful the longer you have it, and never turns into one more thing to manage.

The real difference is what happens over time: it remembers what it's already done, abilities one assistant gains can carry over to the others, and it builds on itself — without turning into one more thing to manage. That's the part the others don't have.

vs. reminder & automation apps
Do exactly what you set up. Forever. No more, no less.
↓
Understands what you meant, and gets better at it.

Reminder apps and tools like IFTTT or Zapier follow the rule you built — and break the moment reality doesn't match it. A Ward assistant works from what you actually want, adapts as things change, and quietly picks up new abilities over time.

vs. a drawer full of apps
A different app for every little thing — each with its own login and learning curve.
↓
One place, plain English, for all of it.

Instead of ten single-purpose apps you have to check, it's one spot where you describe what you want and it's handled. Every assistant lives together, and they can all draw on the same connections you've set up.

vs. a chatbot
Answers when you ask. Silent otherwise.
↓
Works in the background and shows up with the answer.

ChatGPT and the like are great — but you have to remember to ask, every time. A Ward assistant just does its thing on its own and leaves the result waiting for you. No prompt, no open laptop.

The default. Fast, capable, and what nearly everything should use.

High3 runs

A stronger model when the work needs more care than the default gives it.

Max5 runs

Our most capable models, for the jobs you'd want a specialist on.

Standard and High are on every plan, Free included — though at 3 runs a day, a High run uses your whole budget. Max is on Plus and Pro; at 5 runs it wouldn't fit in Free anyway.
